MATRIX OPEN FRAME - REVISION 3

The frame follows the latest sketch: a continuous border, a centered horizontal
and vertical cross, and a shorter left branch for the upper PCB mounting pair.
The lower PCB pair and both lever-connector screw holes share the horizontal
centerline. Four midpoint M3 screws attach it to the matrix. All four factory
corner inserts are exposed for your own magnetic standoffs.

INSERTS AND SCREWS

Six M2.5 heat-set inserts. This design explicitly targets the common ruthex
RX-M2.5x5.7 geometry: 4.6 mm outside diameter, 5.7 mm length, 4.0 mm pilot.
"M2.5" alone does not specify insert outside diameter or length. For another
insert body, check the coupon and adjust the parameters before the full print.

Each pocket is 6.7 mm deep with a 1.3 mm closed floor. The insert seats flush
with the top of its 8 mm post. A small entrance chamfer helps it start straight.
There are no captive hex nuts. The first M3 insert proposal is superseded;
the printed frame has only M2.5 insert pockets for its six electronics screws.

Nominal hardware starting points:
  4 x M3 x 8 mm countersunk screws for the panel midpoint inserts.
    Frame clearance 3.4 mm; 6.4 mm countersink, 1.5 mm deep.
    About 4 mm screw projection below the frame. Check actual panel thread
    depth before tightening; the drawing specifies M3 x 5 inserts.
  4 x M2.5 x 6 mm screws for the PCB's 1.6 mm thickness, no washers assumed.
    Nominal insert engagement 4.4 mm.
  2 x M2.5 x 6 mm screws plus thin washers for the lever connector.
    With assumed 2.2 mm ears and 0.4 mm washers, engagement is 3.4 mm.
    Actual ear thickness/underside seating height were not supplied: verify
    screw length and seating on the fit check before choosing longer screws.

The lever has approximately 3 mm bores, so M2.5 screws give about 0.5 mm
diametral clearance. Its printed insert centers are 19 mm apart. Tighten
gently after checking that all ears and PCB holes seat without bending.

PRINT AND ASSEMBLE

1. Print the sizing coupon with your final material and settings. Set one
   insert into each trial pocket; choose the pocket that installs cleanly
   and holds without cracking. Default frame is the 4.0 mm trial size.
2. Print the electronics fit-check skeleton. Install inserts, then check all
   four PCB holes, both lever ears, underside socket clearance and the actual
   power leads. Some connector/board edges deliberately overhang this sample.
3. Print the full frame flat side down, posts upward, at 100% scale in mm.
   Suggested starting settings: PETG, 0.2 mm layers, 4-5 walls, 5 top/bottom
   layers, 25-35% infill, supports off. The open frame needs no pocket roofs.
4. Heat-set the six inserts before fitting electronics. Keep them vertical
   and flush, then let the plastic cool completely before threading screws.
5. Attach the frame using the four midpoint panel holes. Fit the electronics
   and harness. The open areas provide access to both HUB75 connectors and
   the matrix power connector. Two small border notches accept a supply tie.
6. Install your magnetic standoffs directly in the four factory corner inserts.
   There is no printed material within 25 mm of each corner-hole center.

Nominal closed lever body reaches 21.5 mm above the panel attachment plane;
the schematic levers reach about 22.7 mm. The real IDC plug, ribbon bend and
open lever sweep can require more. Check the assembled hardware before
selecting magnetic standoff height. No physical load or thermal test was done.

ORIENTATION AND CABLES

Match the latest rear-view drawing: controller on left in portrait orientation,
USB/buttons at bottom, HUB75 header at top. Lever connector is to the right,
with four-port end above the centerline and two-port supply end below.
The panel power connector is at upper right; HUB75 INPUT is at lower left.
This view rotates the original rear photograph by 180 degrees.

Planar cable centerline estimates (not measured 3D harness lengths):
  Matrix power:      27.8 mm planned / 50.8 mm available
  Controller power:  53.2 mm planned / 100 mm available
  HUB75 ribbon:     219.1 mm planned / 254 mm available

The nominal slack is useful, but plug takeoffs, wire bends and vertical travel
consume it. Dry-fit actual leads. The ribbon can run around the left of the
PCB through the open areas. The Blender cable collection is hidden by default;
enable it for an illustrative route. It is not a bend-radius simulation.

The PCB edge is nominally 23 mm inward from the left inner plastic edge. That
plastic edge is estimated 12.7 mm from the panel's outside edge. Measure it
if that inset must be exact. Board hole pitch is 19.685 x 40.64 mm in this view,
from Adafruit's PCB source. Lever hole pitch is your approximately 19 mm value.

CHECKS AND REMAINING FIT ASSUMPTIONS

All three exported printable STLs reload as watertight solids with consistent
winding. Each original CSG solid is one connected body. Checks confirm the
six hole centers, centerline alignment, 1.3 mm pocket floors, four corner
keepouts, and no intersection with the modeled underside HUB75 female socket.
The PCB branch edge clears that socket's nominal footprint by about 0.49 mm.

Lever body envelope (42 x 26 x 13.5 mm), ear offset and ear seating are based
on the similar reference, not a full measurement of your exact connector.
Panel connector/capacitor positions come from the photos. The inner frame
edge, insert fit, cable bends, lever sweep and real screw lengths need the
physical checks above. Geometry validation is complete; physical fit is not.

EDITABLE FILES

source/rev3-parameters.json, build_mount_v3.py, csg.py, draw_layout_v3.py.
Install source/requirements.txt in Python, then run build_mount_v3.py and
draw_layout_v3.py. Outputs regenerate in source/output/rev3/.
The generated OpenSCAD files reproduce the same geometry with baked values;
change the JSON and regenerate to change dimensions. STL cleanup only removes
sub-micron CSG slivers introduced by binary STL coordinate rounding.

layout-v3-actual-size.svg is an A4 reference template with electronics/cable
overlays. Print at 100% and verify its 100 mm calibration line. It is not an
insert drill jig. The PNG gives the same layout for on-screen review.
